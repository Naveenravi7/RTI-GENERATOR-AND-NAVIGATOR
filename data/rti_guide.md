# Comprehensive Guide: Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005

The Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005 is a landmark legislation in India that empowers citizens to request information from public authorities, promoting transparency and accountability.

---

## 1. Core Principles of RTI
- **Who can file?** Only Indian Citizens can file an RTI application.
- **What is a Public Authority?** Any authority, body, or institution of self-government established or constituted by the Constitution, by Parliament, by State Legislature, or by notification of the appropriate government (including bodies owned, controlled, or substantially financed by the government).
- **Format of Application**: There is no rigid format. The application can be on plain paper. However, it must contain:
  1. The name and full postal address of the applicant.
  2. Clear, specific, and point-wise questions requesting information.
  3. Proof of payment of application fee (or proof of Below Poverty Line status).
  4. A declaration of Indian citizenship.

---

## 2. Timeline for Receiving Information
- **Standard Request**: Information must be provided within **30 days** of receipt of the application.
- **Life or Liberty**: If the information sought concerns the life or liberty of a person, it must be provided within **48 hours**.
- **Assistant PIO (APIO)**: If the application is submitted to an APIO instead of a PIO, **5 days** are added to the timeline (making it **35 days**).
- **Third Party Information**: If a third party is involved, the timeline is extended to **40 days**.

---

## 3. Fees and Payment Methods
- **Application Fee**: The standard fee for filing an RTI application is **Rs. 10/-**.
- **Fee Exemption**: Citizens who belong to the **Below Poverty Line (BPL)** category are **exempt** from paying the application fee. They must attach a copy of their BPL Card/Certificate as proof.
- **Payment Modes**:
  - **Online**: Net banking, Credit/Debit cards, UPI on government RTI portals (e.g., rtionline.gov.in for Central Government).
  - **Offline**:
    - **Indian Postal Order (IPO)**: The most common and reliable method. Draw in favor of the designated accounts officer of the public authority.
    - **Demand Draft (DD) / Banker's Cheque**.
    - **Court Fee Stamp**: Accepted in certain states (e.g., Maharashtra) pasted directly on the application.
    - **Cash**: Payment at the office against a formal receipt.
- **Additional Costs**: If photocopies or documents are provided, additional fees may apply:
  - Rs. 2/- per page (A4/A3 size).
  - Actual cost/price for larger sizes, models, or samples.
  - Rs. 50/- per CD/floppy disk.
  - Inspection of records: No fee for the first hour; Rs. 5/- for each subsequent hour.

---

## 4. Drafting Effective RTI Questions
To avoid rejection or vague answers, applications should follow these guidelines:
- **Be Objective**: Ask for records, files, work orders, circulars, or reports. Do not ask for opinions, reasons, or justifications (e.g., ask "Provide the rules under which X was done" rather than "Why did you do X?").
- **Ask Point-wise**: Enumerate your queries clearly (e.g., Point 1, Point 2, Point 3).
- **Limit Scope**: Keep questions specific to a particular event, timeline, or file. Broad, sweeping queries are often rejected or delayed.
- **Request specific formats**: State whether you want photocopies, certified copies, inspection of records, or digital copies.

---

## 5. Exemptions from Disclosure (Section 8)
Public authorities are not obligated to give information that falls under **Section 8(1)** of the RTI Act. Key exemptions include:
- **Section 8(1)(a)**: Information that would prejudicially affect the sovereignty and integrity of India, security, strategic, scientific, or economic interests of the State.
- **Section 8(1)(b)**: Information forbidden to be published by any court of law or tribunal.
- **Section 8(1)(c)**: Information causing a breach of privilege of Parliament or State Legislature.
- **Section 8(1)(d)**: Commercial confidence, trade secrets, or intellectual property, unless larger public interest warrants disclosure.
- **Section 8(1)(e)**: Information available to a person in their fiduciary relationship, unless larger public interest warrants disclosure.
- **Section 8(1)(f)**: Information received in confidence from foreign governments.
- **Section 8(1)(g)**: Information that would endanger the life or physical safety of any person.
- **Section 8(1)(h)**: Information that would impede the process of investigation, apprehension, or prosecution of offenders.
- **Section 8(1)(j)**: Personal information that has no relation to any public activity or interest, or which would cause unwarranted invasion of the privacy of the individual (unless there is a larger public interest).

---

## 6. First and Second Appeals
If you do not receive a response within the timeline, or if the information provided is incomplete, misleading, or rejected, you have the right to appeal:
1. **First Appeal**:
   - **To whom**: Filed with the **First Appellate Authority (FAA)**, who is senior in rank to the PIO in the same public authority.
   - **Timeline**: Must be filed within **30 days** of receiving the PIO's decision, or within 30 days from the expiry of the response period (if no reply was received).
   - **Resolution**: The FAA must dispose of the appeal within **30 days** (extendable up to **45 days** with written reasons).
2. **Second Appeal**:
   - **To whom**: Filed with the **Central Information Commission (CIC)** (for central authorities) or **State Information Commission (SIC)** (for state authorities).
   - **Timeline**: Must be filed within **90 days** from the date on which the FAA decision was made or should have been received.
